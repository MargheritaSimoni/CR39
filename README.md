# CR39 Nuclear Track Detector Analysis

This repository contains a Monte Carlo simulation framework for the study of neutron interactions in CR-39 (PADC) solid-state nuclear track detectors, published in [1].

The simulations are designed to quantify the formation length of particle tracks inside CR-39 prior to chemical etching, with particular focus on the production and transport of recoil nuclei generated by neutron interactions across a broad energy range.

## Project Overview

The simulation framework models neutron irradiation of CR-39 dosimeters and records the microscopic energy deposition and transport properties of secondary charged particles generated inside the detector material.

The primary goals of the project are:

Estimation of track formation lengths in CR-39
Characterization of recoil particle transport
Evaluation of energy deposition mechanisms
Investigation of neutron energy dependence
Estimation of detectable track densities under different threshold conditions

## Simulation Setup

Targets with the same physical dimensions as the experimental CR-39 dosimeters are placed in vacuum and irradiated using monochromatic neutron beams perpendicular to the detector surface.

The beam is assumed to:

uniformly cover the entire detector area
have no divergence
impinge normally on the flat dosimeter face

The detector material is implemented as a custom-defined PADC material with:

Stoichiometry: C12H18O7
Density: 1.15 g/cm³

n.b.: Material for CR39 is defined through ncrystal .ncmat file to implement tests in thermal energy range, but final results are reported for higher energies, where NCrystal stops overwriting cross sections. Therefore, cross section models are defined as in the geant4 physics list QGSP_BIC_HP

## Secondary Particle Scoring

Scoring is implemented through custom sensitive detector and hit classes that record, for each transport step inside the detector:

secondary particle type
deposited energy
step length

These quantities are accumulated to obtain:

total deposited energy
total track length

The simulated secondary particles primarily consist of recoil nuclei originating from the natural isotopes contained in CR-39:

Hydrogen
Carbon
Oxygen

At higher neutron energies, the simulations may also include products of inelastic nuclear reactions.

## Build and running instructions

Create a build directory and compile the project:

mkdir build
cd build
cmake ..
make

Run the simulation as:

Example execution:

./exampleB4c.cc -m macros/monoGPS.mac
